# ImpactProof's Responsible AI Statement

*Customer/investor-facing. Distilled from `docs/ethics_framework.md`'s full internal audit
(Laudon Ch.4) — that document has the reasoning and the file-level citations; this one is
the pitch-ready version. Last reviewed 2026-08-04.*

## The one rule everything else follows from

**No AI feature in ImpactProof may invent, estimate, or impute a number, date, or fact you
didn't give it.** Not a guideline — a machine-checked rule. Every AI-drafted statement is
scanned for every numeral, year, and percentage it contains, and each one is verified against
your own submission before it's ever shown to you. If an AI draft introduces a fact you never
supplied, it is withheld — you see a plain message saying so, never a confident-sounding
number that was never yours.

## Why the score isn't the AI's opinion

Your Confidence and Clarity scores are computed by a deterministic rules engine, not
generated by a language model. The same submission produces the same score every time,
from every reviewer, forever — because it's arithmetic against a published rubric (anchored
in USAID, OECD-DAC, Bond, FCDO, and World Bank standards), not a language model's judgment
call. AI in ImpactProof narrates, questions, and drafts *around* that score — it never sets
it, and it never gets to override it.

## What "responsible" actually means here, concretely

- **Every AI draft is traceable.** The rules that fired to produce your score are logged and
  citable — you can see exactly why a criterion scored what it did, not just that it did.
- **A wrong-feeling rule can be formally disputed** — disagreement is a first-class product
  path, not a support ticket black hole.
- **Different organisation types are held to honest, different bars — and that's disclosed,
  not hidden.** A community-based organisation and an international NGO don't have the same
  access to verification infrastructure; scoring them against an identical threshold would
  make the tool useless to the organisations that need a rigor check most. The bar used is
  always labeled on your results, never silently applied.
- **The people your reports are about are protected even from a "correct" score.** A report
  describing programming that put a vulnerable population at risk cannot reach a passing
  badge no matter how well-written it is — do-no-harm and child-safeguarding checks hard-gate
  the result, and cannot be scored around.
- **Your data stays yours.** Uploaded documents are processed in memory and never stored.
  Free-text content — your result statements, your evidence descriptions — never enters any
  analytics warehouse, ever, by construction, not by policy. You can permanently delete your
  saved history at any time.

## The honest gaps

We don't claim a finished system. Today: there's no self-service "export everything you have
on me" button yet (support-request only); we haven't yet built a formal check for whether our
tiered thresholds are calibrated correctly at real scale, because we don't have that scale
yet. Both are tracked, both are visible in the internal version of this document, neither is
hidden from this one.

## The short version

ImpactProof's AI is built to be the thing that questions your report, not the thing that
writes it for you. If it can't verify something, it says so — it never guesses on your
behalf.
